# UI

The site is a portfolio styled as a retro first-person point-and-click dungeon crawler. The planned design:

- **Rendering:** the room is drawn on an HTML5 `<canvas>`, not as DOM elements. It uses a dark background with off-white, 8-bit pixel-art elements. Walls are sparse pixelated brick patterns. Text uses a medium 8-bit retro font.
  - Implemented in `src/ViewPane/`: the room canvas draws at a fixed 320×240 (`VIEW_WIDTH`/`VIEW_HEIGHT`). The pane is split into three areas, laid out as a CSS grid in a `.view-pane-frame` wrapper, 457×377 canvas pixels inside the frame:
    - the room in the upper left, 70% of the pane's width
    - a lower panel under the room, 320×132 (`LOWER_PANEL_WIDTH`/`LOWER_PANEL_HEIGHT`, `.view-pane-lower`): as tall as the side panel is wide, to keep it compact
    - a side panel down the right, 132×377 (`SIDE_PANEL_WIDTH`/`SIDE_PANEL_HEIGHT`, `.view-pane-side`)

    The panels are canvases too, hidden from assistive tech. The lower panel holds the navigation panel (see **Navigation**); the side panel is filled with the background for now. CSS scales the whole pane to the largest size that fits inside 80vw × 80vh, centred, with `image-rendering: pixelated`. The wrapper has a pixel-art frame (`src/ViewPane/frame.svg`, applied as a `border-image`). The frame has stepped corners, a black outer outline, an off-white band with a worn bevel, and a black inner outline. It's 5 frame pixels thick, and each frame pixel is sized to match one canvas pixel on screen. The areas are divided by 5-pixel bands in the same colours, bevelled on both sides (`.view-pane-divider`); the vertical band runs unbroken past the end of the horizontal one. The panel sizes allow for the bands, so the room keeps exactly 70% of the width (and about 64% of the height). Only the room canvas has cursor zones and click handling; the frame, bands and panels aren't part of them. Drawing coordinates therefore stay the same at every window size.
- **Scene drawing:** pure modules in `src/scene/` return lists of `Pixel`s (`{ x, y, colour }`), and `drawScene` in `src/scene/draw.ts` paints them onto the canvas. `ViewPane` calls it once on mount. The modules are grouped by what they draw:
  - `core/`: the camera and projection, line rasterising, palette, pixel font, seeded randomness and the `Pixel` type. Nothing here imports from the other folders.
  - `navigation/`: headings and the places the visitor can stand, and the navigation panel's compass and direction buttons.
  - `panels/`: logic shared by the display panels beside the room (the navigation panel and the side panel), such as `clearPanel`.
  - `room/`: the floor plan, walls, visibility and the room's own detail.
  - `mirror/`: the mirror's frame, preview, ripple, curtain pull and logo plaque. `pixels.ts` projects them all onto the mirror's wall and maps canvas positions back to it.
  - `signpost/`: the welcome sign.

  Colours are in `src/scene/core/palette.ts`. Text uses the 5×7 bitmap font in `src/scene/core/pixelFont.ts` rather than canvas text, which would be anti-aliased; add glyphs there as needed. Randomness (such as weathering) uses the seeded `createRandom` so every render is identical.
- **Room and view:** the room's floor plan, places, camera, visibility, detail, object placement and draw order are in [layout.md](layout.md).
- **Welcome signpost:** `src/scene/signpost/signpost.ts`. A double-outlined "WELCOME" board, centred vertically in the view, on a single post that sticks up `POST_PROTRUSION` pixels above it above a fading ground line. The board is tilted by shifting pixels along `BOARD_SLOPE` rather than by canvas rotation, which keeps it crisp. It's worn: a chipped top-right corner, a crack, and outline and text pixels randomly dropped or dimmed. How it's placed and scaled in the world is in [layout.md](layout.md#objects).
- **Navigation:** the visitor starts facing a welcome sign. Clicking the left or right edge of the view turns the visitor, and clicking the centre moves them forward when forward movement is possible.
  - Turning is implemented in `ViewPane`. The visitor's `heading` (`north`, `west`, `south`, `east`; `src/scene/navigation/heading.ts`) is exposed as `data-heading` on the canvas. Clicking a turn zone changes the heading by a quarter turn and pans the camera yaw over `TURN_DURATION_MS` (250 ms) with ease-in-out, redrawing every animation frame. Clicks during a pan are ignored. With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the view snaps to the new heading.
  - Moving: from the `start` facing west the visitor can step forward into the `nook`; from the nook facing east they step back. Places and exits are described in [layout.md](layout.md#places). `ViewPane` tracks the place (`data-place` on the canvas) and animates moves over `MOVE_DURATION_MS` (350 ms) with the same ease-in-out, interpolating the eye position. Clicks during any animation are ignored.
  - **Navigation panel:** the lower panel shows a compass and four direction buttons, drawn as pixel art by `drawNavigationPanel` (`src/scene/navigation/navigationPanel.ts`).
    - The compass (`compassPixels` in `src/scene/navigation/compass.ts`) is a rose that turns with the camera, so the letter for the way the visitor faces sits at the top under a fixed marker. Only the top of the rose shows: an arc of its ring and the letters within `COMPASS_WINDOW` (60°) of the top. At rest that's just the faced letter; during a turn it slides away round the arc as the next one slides in. Each letter's direction is a yaw (N 0, W π/2, S π, E −π/2), drawn at `letterYaw − yaw` from the top. Letters move round the arc but stay upright. The faced letter is bright and any other worn. `ViewPane` redraws the panel on every turn and move frame, so the rose turns in step with the view.
    - The buttons (`src/scene/navigation/navButtons.ts`) sit to the compass's right in a keyboard's arrow-key layout: up on top, and down in a row with left and right. Left and right turn the view, as the room's turn zones do. Up steps forward, as a centre click does. Down steps back to the place behind without turning (`backFrom` in `src/scene/navigation/places.ts`), so from the start facing east it leads into the nook. Up and down are dimmed and do nothing when there's nowhere to go. `ViewPane` hit-tests clicks with `navButtonAt`; the hand cursor shows over usable buttons and the blocked cursor over dimmed ones. Like room clicks, button clicks wait for any animation to finish.
- **Cursor:** a large pixelated pointer that changes by hover region: a turn arrow at the left and right edges, and a forward arrow in the centre when the visitor can move forward.
  - Implemented in `src/ViewPane/cursors.ts`. The left and right 15% of the pane (`EDGE_ZONE_FRACTION`) show turn arrows and the middle shows the forward arrow. Arrows are flat shapes lying on the floor, seen from above at 45° with the same forced perspective as the room. `projectArrow` rasterises floor-coordinate polygons into 16×16 pixel maps: `#` is the top face and `+` is the visible side face, shown in the worn colour. The far end is foreshortened by cos 45° and narrowed by `PERSPECTIVE`. Outlines are generated, and the maps are drawn at 2× as 32px SVG data-URI CSS cursors. The centre shows the forward arrow only when there's somewhere to move; otherwise it shows the blocked cursor (`BLOCKED_MAP` / `BLOCKED_CURSOR`): an open hand, fingers spread, lying on the floor in the same projection as the arrows. Its fingers fan out slightly so each lands on its own pixel column once foreshortened.
  - At the start facing north and not mid-animation, over the welcome sign's board the cursor becomes a pixelated pointing hand (`HAND_MAP` / `HAND_CURSOR`). The hand is upright, not projected onto the floor, and its hotspot is the fingertip (`pixelMapToCursor` takes an optional hotspot in map pixels). The hover area is the rectangle from the tilted board's lower-left corner to its upper-right corner (`signBoardBounds` in `src/scene/signpost/signpost.ts`). `ViewPane` converts the mouse position to canvas pixels using fractions of the canvas's on-screen size, so the area scales with the pane.
  - In the nook facing west and not mid-animation, the same hand shows over the mirror's glass. The frame and its ornaments don't count. The hover area is the glass's arch-topped shape: `ViewPane` checks it with `screenToMirror` (`src/scene/mirror/pixels.ts`), the same test a click uses.
  - In the same view, the hand also shows over the curtain pull beside the mirror. Its hover area is `PULL_HIT` (`src/scene/mirror/pull.ts`): the handle and the lower cord, padded so the one-pixel cord is easy to hit. `ViewPane` finds the point on the wall with `screenToWall` and checks it with `isOnPull`.
- **Mirror:** on the nook's back wall, one step west of the start (drawn; see [layout.md](layout.md#objects)). The glass shows a preview of one of several profiles: GitHub, then LinkedIn (`MIRROR_SITES` in `src/ViewPane/mirrorSites.ts`). Clicking the glass plays a water-ripple animation that spreads from the click point, then redirects the browser to the profile it shows. Clicking the curtain pull to its right tugs the pull and shimmers the glass over to the next profile. A plaque below the pull shows the current profile's logo.
  - Each preview is a committed screenshot of the profile page, because browsers can't draw a cross-origin page into a canvas and these sites can't be framed. The screenshots are `src/assets/github-preview.png` and `src/assets/linkedin-preview.png`. `npm run capture:preview` (`scripts/capture-preview.mjs`, using Playwright) retakes them all: a 390-wide phone viewport in dark mode, cropped to the glass's aspect ratio, with any sign-in prompt dismissed. Its `PAGES` list should match `MIRROR_SITES`.
  - LinkedIn has no dark mode, so its page is marked `invert`. Before the screenshot, CSS inverts the page's colours and inverts pictures back, so the page goes dark like the scene while photos stay normal.
  - `ViewPane` loads every site's screenshot on mount. Each one and scales it to cover a grid of `PREVIEW_WIDTH` × `PREVIEW_HEIGHT` cells (`src/scene/mirror/preview.ts`), one per canvas pixel from the nook. `quantisePreview` maps it to the scene's four tones with 4×4 ordered dithering, treating near-black as background.
  - `previewPixels` (`src/scene/mirror/pixels.ts`) projects each glass cell onto the mirror's wall and draws it as a block sized by its depth, so it shows one pixel per cell from the nook and smaller from further away. Until a site's screenshot loads, or if it fails, its glass is empty.
  - The ripple is built. `screenToMirror` (`src/scene/mirror/pixels.ts`) turns the click into a point on the glass by casting a ray to the mirror's wall. Clicks on the frame or ornaments, off the glass (`isOnGlass` in `src/scene/mirror/frame.ts`), do nothing. `src/scene/mirror/ripple.ts` sends out three rings, `EMIT_INTERVAL` apart, in mirror-local units. A ring's radius is `SPEED · age^GROWTH`, so each ring speeds up as it spreads and the outer rings are further apart. A ring's strength falls from 1 at the click to 0 at `1 / FADE_RATE` of the way to the glass's furthest point from it. With `FADE_RATE` 2 that's half-way, so rings fade out before the far edge. Strong rings are drawn worn, weaker ones dim, and the weakest lose more and more pixels until they vanish. `rippleDuration` is when the last ring fades, so clicks near an edge ripple for longer. `ripplePixels` projects the rings like the mirror, and they're clipped to the glass.
  - While a ripple plays, the preview is sampled through `rippleOffset`, so the picture under each ring is pushed outward just behind its crest and inward just ahead of it. The rings are drawn over the picture.
  - While the ripple plays the view counts as animating, so turns, moves and the hand wait for it. When it ends, `openUrl` sends the browser to the profile in the same tab. With reduced motion (or no canvas), a click on the glass goes straight to the profile without a ripple.
  - **Curtain pull:** a cord hanging from the ceiling to the right of the mirror, ending in an acorn-shaped handle (`src/scene/mirror/pull.ts`; see [layout.md](layout.md#objects)). A click moves to the next site, wrapping after the last, and plays a `SWITCH_DURATION_MS` (900 ms) animation in `ViewPane`:
    - The pull drops 0.1 mirror units over the first 150 ms, with its cord stretching, then springs back by 600 ms.
    - The logo plaque below the pull sinks into the wall over the first 200 ms, turns on its vertical axis to the next site's logo by 650 ms (the logos swap when it's edge-on, half-way through the turn), then pushes back out by 900 ms (`plaqueAt` in `ViewPane`).
    - From 150 ms the glass shimmers from the old preview to the new one (`shimmerTone` in `src/scene/mirror/preview.ts`). Each cell switches at its own moment, from a sweep down from the top left plus random jitter. Cells close to their switch flash a tone brighter, so a sparkle passes over the glass as the picture changes.
    - Input waits until it's done, as with other animations. With reduced motion (or no canvas), the site changes at once.
    - The canvas's `data-site` attribute names the current site.
  - **Logo plaque:** a stone block set into the wall below the pull, its face showing the current site's logo (`src/scene/mirror/plaque.ts`; see [layout.md](layout.md#objects)). Each site's logo is a hand-drawn 16×16 pixel map (`src/ViewPane/siteLogos.ts`): the Octocat and LinkedIn's "in". It isn't clickable.
