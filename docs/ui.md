# UI

The site is a portfolio styled as a retro first-person point-and-click dungeon crawler. The planned design:

- **Rendering:** the room is drawn on an HTML5 `<canvas>`, not as DOM elements. It uses a dark background with off-white, 8-bit pixel-art elements. Walls are sparse pixelated brick patterns. Text uses a medium 8-bit retro font.
  - Implemented in `src/ViewPane/`: the canvas draws at a fixed 320×240 (`VIEW_WIDTH`/`VIEW_HEIGHT`). CSS scales it to the largest 4:3 size that fits inside 80vw × 80vh, centred, with `image-rendering: pixelated`. The canvas sits in a `.view-pane-frame` wrapper with a pixel-art frame (`src/ViewPane/frame.svg`, applied as a `border-image`). The frame has stepped corners, a black outer outline, an off-white band with a worn bevel, and a black inner outline. It's 5 frame pixels thick, and each frame pixel is sized to match one canvas pixel on screen. The frame is outside the canvas, so it isn't part of the cursor zones. Drawing coordinates therefore stay the same at every window size.
- **Scene drawing:** pure modules in `src/scene/` return lists of `Pixel`s (`{ x, y, colour }`), and `drawScene` in `src/scene/draw.ts` paints them onto the canvas. `ViewPane` calls it once on mount. Colours are in `src/scene/palette.ts`. Text uses the 5×7 bitmap font in `src/scene/pixelFont.ts` rather than canvas text, which would be anti-aliased; add glyphs there as needed. Randomness (such as weathering) uses the seeded `createRandom` so every render is identical.
- **Room and view:** the room's floor plan, places, camera, visibility, detail, object placement and draw order are in [layout.md](layout.md).
- **Welcome signpost:** `src/scene/signpost.ts`. A double-outlined "WELCOME" board, centred vertically in the view, on a single post that sticks up `POST_PROTRUSION` pixels above it above a fading ground line. The board is tilted by shifting pixels along `BOARD_SLOPE` rather than by canvas rotation, which keeps it crisp. It's worn: a chipped top-right corner, a crack, and outline and text pixels randomly dropped or dimmed. How it's placed and scaled in the world is in [layout.md](layout.md#objects).
- **Navigation:** the visitor starts facing a welcome sign. Clicking the left or right edge of the view turns the visitor, and clicking the centre moves them forward when forward movement is possible.
  - Turning is implemented in `ViewPane`. The visitor's `heading` (`north`, `west`, `south`, `east`; `src/scene/heading.ts`) is exposed as `data-heading` on the canvas. Clicking a turn zone changes the heading by a quarter turn and pans the camera yaw over `TURN_DURATION_MS` (250 ms) with ease-in-out, redrawing every animation frame. Clicks during a pan are ignored. With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the view snaps to the new heading.
  - Moving: from the `start` facing west the visitor can step forward into the `nook`; from the nook facing east they step back. Places and exits are described in [layout.md](layout.md#places). `ViewPane` tracks the place (`data-place` on the canvas) and animates moves over `MOVE_DURATION_MS` (350 ms) with the same ease-in-out, interpolating the eye position. Clicks during any animation are ignored.
- **Cursor:** a large pixelated pointer that changes by hover region: a turn arrow at the left and right edges, and a forward arrow in the centre when the visitor can move forward.
  - Implemented in `src/ViewPane/cursors.ts`. The left and right 15% of the pane (`EDGE_ZONE_FRACTION`) show turn arrows and the middle shows the forward arrow. Arrows are flat shapes lying on the floor, seen from above at 45° with the same forced perspective as the room. `projectArrow` rasterises floor-coordinate polygons into 16×16 pixel maps: `#` is the top face and `+` is the visible side face, shown in the worn colour. The far end is foreshortened by cos 45° and narrowed by `PERSPECTIVE`. Outlines are generated, and the maps are drawn at 2× as 32px SVG data-URI CSS cursors. The centre shows the forward arrow only when there's somewhere to move; otherwise it shows the blocked cursor (`BLOCKED_MAP` / `BLOCKED_CURSOR`): an open hand, fingers spread, lying on the floor in the same projection as the arrows. Its fingers fan out slightly so each lands on its own pixel column once foreshortened.
  - At the start facing north and not mid-animation, over the welcome sign's board the cursor becomes a pixelated pointing hand (`HAND_MAP` / `HAND_CURSOR`). The hand is upright, not projected onto the floor, and its hotspot is the fingertip (`pixelMapToCursor` takes an optional hotspot in map pixels). The hover area is the rectangle from the tilted board's lower-left corner to its upper-right corner (`signBoardBounds` in `src/scene/signpost.ts`). `ViewPane` converts the mouse position to canvas pixels using fractions of the canvas's on-screen size, so the area scales with the pane.
  - In the nook facing west and not mid-animation, the same hand shows over the mirror. Its hover area is the bounding rectangle of the mirror's drawn pixels in that view (`mirrorBounds` in `src/scene/room.ts`).
- **Mirror:** on the nook's back wall, one step west of the start (drawn; see [layout.md](layout.md#objects)). A dial at its top has left and right buttons that rotate it through site names (LinkedIn, GitHub, …). Clicking the mirror plays a water-ripple animation that spreads from the click point to the mirror's edges, then redirects the browser to the selected profile. The dial and redirect aren't built yet.
  - The ripple is built. `screenToMirror` (`src/scene/room.ts`) turns the click into a point on the glass by casting a ray to the mirror's wall. Clicks on the frame or ornaments, off the glass (`isOnGlass` in `src/scene/mirror.ts`), do nothing. `src/scene/ripple.ts` sends out three rings, `EMIT_INTERVAL` apart, in mirror-local units. A ring's radius is `SPEED · age^GROWTH`, so each ring speeds up as it spreads and the outer rings are further apart. A ring's strength falls from 1 at the click to 0 at the glass's furthest point from it. Strong rings are drawn worn, weaker ones dim, and the weakest lose more and more pixels until they vanish. `rippleDuration` is when the last ring fades, so clicks near an edge ripple for longer. `ripplePixels` projects the rings like the mirror, and they're clipped to the glass.
  - While the ripple plays the view counts as animating, so turns, moves and the hand wait for it. With reduced motion no ripple is shown.
