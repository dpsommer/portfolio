# UI

The site is a portfolio styled as a retro first-person point-and-click dungeon crawler. The planned design:

- **Rendering:** the room is drawn on an HTML5 `<canvas>`, not as DOM elements. It uses a dark background with off-white, 8-bit pixel-art elements. Walls are sparse pixelated brick patterns. Text uses a medium 8-bit retro font.
  - Implemented in `src/ViewPane/`: the canvas draws at a fixed 320×240 (`VIEW_WIDTH`/`VIEW_HEIGHT`). CSS scales it to the largest 4:3 size that fits inside 80vw × 80vh, centred, with `image-rendering: pixelated`. The canvas sits in a `.view-pane-frame` wrapper with a pixel-art frame (`src/ViewPane/frame.svg`, applied as a `border-image`). The frame has stepped corners, a black outer outline, an off-white band with a worn bevel, and a black inner outline. It's 5 frame pixels thick, and each frame pixel is sized to match one canvas pixel on screen. The frame is outside the canvas, so it isn't part of the cursor zones. Drawing coordinates therefore stay the same at every window size.
- **Scene drawing:** pure modules in `src/scene/` return lists of `Pixel`s (`{ x, y, colour }`), and `drawScene` in `src/scene/draw.ts` paints them onto the canvas. `ViewPane` calls it once on mount. Colours are in `src/scene/palette.ts`. Text uses the 5×7 bitmap font in `src/scene/pixelFont.ts` rather than canvas text, which would be anti-aliased; add glyphs there as needed. Randomness (such as weathering) uses the seeded `createRandom` so every render is identical.
- **Room:** the room is a box in world space, seen through a minimal camera (`src/scene/camera.ts`). The camera sits at the origin and only turns about the vertical axis (yaw, in radians anticlockwise from north).
  - Units: the room is 2 wide (`ROOM_HALF_WIDTH` 1). Floor and ceiling are at ∓0.75, a 3:4 ratio matching the canvas, so facing north the wall edges run into the view's corners. The focal length is `FOCAL_RATIO` (0.8) × half the canvas width. The far (north) wall's distance `FAR_Z` is derived so it fills `FAR_WALL_SCALE` (0.45) of the view, giving the classic dungeon-crawler "narrowing box". The back wall is at `BACK_Z` (−1), behind the start position, far enough that facing south shows it framed by its edges.
  - `projectSegment` rotates world segments into camera space (`toCamera`), clips them to the `NEAR` plane and projects them. The box is convex and seen from inside, so nothing needs occlusion.
  - `src/scene/room.ts` builds all detail in world space: the 12 box edges as broken lines in `FOREGROUND_WORN`, sparse bricks on all four walls drawn as just their top and left edges, and scattered flagstone edges on a grid of 1/3-unit floor tiles, both in `FOREGROUND_DIM`. Each brick, floor edge and edge gap pattern is chosen with `hashRandom`/`hashSeed` (`src/scene/random.ts`) from its own world coordinates, not one sequential stream, so detail stays put while the view turns instead of shimmering. The segments are built once; `roomPixels(width, height, yaw)` only projects them.
- **Draw order:** `drawScene(ctx, yaw)` fills the background, then draws the room, then background-coloured backing pixels for objects (`signpostBackingPixels`) so the room doesn't show through their outline-only art, then the objects themselves.
- **Welcome signpost:** `src/scene/signpost.ts`. A double-outlined "WELCOME" board, centred vertically in the view, on a single post that sticks up `POST_PROTRUSION` pixels above it above a fading ground line. The board is tilted by shifting pixels along `BOARD_SLOPE` rather than by canvas rotation, which keeps it crisp. It's worn: a chipped top-right corner, a crack, and outline and text pixels randomly dropped or dimmed.
  - The sprite is drawn for the north view. It's anchored in the world at `SIGN_POSITION`, and when the view turns `signOffset` (`src/scene/draw.ts`) slides the sprite sideways by its anchor's projected position, without rescaling, so it moves out of view. It isn't drawn once the anchor is behind the camera.
- **Navigation:** the visitor starts facing a welcome sign. Clicking the left or right edge of the view turns the visitor, and clicking the centre moves them forward when forward movement is possible.
  - Turning is implemented in `ViewPane`. The visitor's `heading` (`north`, `west`, `south`, `east`; `src/scene/heading.ts`) is exposed as `data-heading` on the canvas. Clicking a turn zone changes the heading by a quarter turn and pans the camera yaw over `TURN_DURATION_MS` (250 ms) with ease-in-out, redrawing every animation frame. Clicks during a pan are ignored. With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the view snaps to the new heading.
  - Facing west, the west wall is ahead, the back wall shows on the left and the far wall and sign are out of view; facing east mirrors this, and facing south looks at the back wall.
- **Cursor:** a large pixelated pointer that changes by hover region: a turn arrow at the left and right edges, and a forward arrow in the centre when the visitor can move forward.
  - Implemented in `src/ViewPane/cursors.ts`. The left and right 15% of the pane (`EDGE_ZONE_FRACTION`) show turn arrows and the middle shows the forward arrow. Arrows are flat shapes lying on the floor, seen from above at 45° with the same forced perspective as the room. `projectArrow` rasterises floor-coordinate polygons into 16×16 pixel maps: `#` is the top face and `+` is the visible side face, shown in the worn colour. The far end is foreshortened by cos 45° and narrowed by `PERSPECTIVE`. Outlines are generated, and the maps are drawn at 2× as 32px SVG data-URI CSS cursors. The centre always shows the forward arrow until navigation state exists.
  - Facing north and not mid-turn, over the welcome sign's board the cursor becomes a pixelated pointing hand (`HAND_MAP` / `HAND_CURSOR`). The hand is upright, not projected onto the floor, and its hotspot is the fingertip (`pixelMapToCursor` takes an optional hotspot in map pixels). The hover area is the rectangle from the tilted board's lower-left corner to its upper-right corner (`signBoardBounds` in `src/scene/signpost.ts`). `ViewPane` converts the mouse position to canvas pixels using fractions of the canvas's on-screen size, so the area scales with the pane.
- **Mirror:** on the left wall, one space from the start. A dial at its top has left and right buttons that rotate it through site names (LinkedIn, GitHub, …). Clicking the mirror plays a water-ripple animation that spreads from the click point to the mirror's edges, then redirects the browser to the selected profile.
